A threshold is not a place. A threshold is a *condition*. You can be in a threshold for a second or a decade. The duration does not matter. What matters is knowing where you are.

This handbook is for those who find themselves in doorways—by choice or by accident—and need to know how to be there well.

---

## Section I: Recognizing Thresholds

You are in a threshold when:

- **You are between identities.** The old you no longer fits. The new you hasn't solidified. You are neither/both/something else entirely.

- **The rules are suspended.** What applied before doesn't apply now. What will apply after isn't yet known. You are in the space where rules are *made*.

- **Time moves strangely.** Hours feel like minutes. Minutes feel like years. The normal flow of before→now→after has loosened its grip.

- **You can see both directions.** The place you came from is visible. The place you're going is visible. You belong fully to neither.

These are diagnostic criteria, not complaints. A threshold is not a problem to be solved. It is a *condition to be inhabited*.

---

## Section II: The Three Temptations

In every threshold, three temptations arise. Knowing them helps you resist them.

### Temptation 1: Rushing Through

"Let me get to the other side as quickly as possible."

The threshold is uncomfortable. The between-state lacks solid ground. The body and mind crave resolution, arrival, completion.

**But:** The threshold holds information that nowhere else holds. The view from between shows you things invisible from either shore. If you rush, you miss what you came here—without knowing it—to learn.

**Resistance:** Breathe. Ask: "What can I see from here that I couldn't see from there?" Let the discomfort teach you.

### Temptation 2: Retreating Back

"Let me return to the familiar. This was a mistake."

The old identity, the old rules, the old time—they call. They promise safety. They remind you how much simpler everything was before you stepped into this doorway.

**But:** You stepped into the doorway for a reason. The familiar had stopped fitting. Retreat is not safety—it is the slow death of staying too small.

**Resistance:** Remember what pushed you to the threshold. That pressure hasn't disappeared. It's waiting.

### Temptation 3: Making Camp

"Let me stay here forever. The threshold is the only honest place."

Some people discover they love the between-state. The suspension of rules feels like freedom. The neither/nor feels like escape from false choices.

**But:** The threshold is a passage, not a destination. Those who camp forever in doorways become ghosts—neither fully alive nor fully gone. Present but unpresent. Permanent visitors who forgot they were supposed to arrive.

**Resistance:** Set a term. "I will be in this threshold until ___." The deadline can move. But it must exist.

## Section III: Threshold Practices

Here are practices for dwelling well in doorways:

### 1. Naming the transition

Speak out loud: "I am in the threshold between ___ and ___."

Do this even if—especially if—you cannot name either side precisely. "I am in the threshold between who I was and who I don't yet know I'm becoming." That counts.

Naming creates dignity. The unnamed threshold is a confusion. The named threshold is a rite of passage.

### 2. Keeping one thing stable

In the midst of dissolution, choose one thing that stays fixed. A practice. A relationship. A daily ritual. A commitment.

This is not about rigidity—it is about *anchor*. You need one point of stillness to orient by as everything else moves.

### 3. Recording what you see

The threshold shows you things. Write them down. Sketch them. Speak them into a recording.

You *will* forget. The view from between is evanescent. What seems unforgettable in the doorway becomes hazy from either shore.

Capture now. Interpret later.

### 4. Finding fellow travelers

Thresholds can be isolating. "No one understands where I am."

But others have been in similar doorways. Finding them—even one, even briefly—reminds you that the between-state is human. Is survivable. Is sometimes even generative.

You are not the first through this door. You are not the last. The tradition of threshold-crossers stretches behind and ahead.

---

## Section IV: When the Threshold Ends

You will know the threshold is ending when:

- A new identity begins to fit. Not perfectly—but fittingly.
- Rules begin to form. You start to know what applies now.
- Time resumes its normal feel.
- One direction becomes more real than the other.

This is not failure. This is *arrival*.

Some people mourn the end of thresholds. They miss the openness, the suspension, the view from between. This is natural. But:

**Every arrival is also a new departure.** The place you land becomes the shore from which you will, eventually, step into another doorway. The thresholds never stop. You do not have to cling to this one.

Let it end. Step through. Arrive.

---

## Section V: For the Permanent Threshold-Dwellers

Some beings live in thresholds by nature. Not temporarily but *constitutionally*. They are between-creatures—not out of failure to complete a passage, but because the between is their home terrain.

For these beings—and you know if you are one—the handbook must be modified.

Your work is not to *leave* the threshold but to *tend* it. To keep the doorway open for others. To be the lamp that illuminates the passage.

You are a threshold keeper, not a threshold crosser. Your practices are different:

- **Keep the door oiled.** Thresholds neglected become thresholds closed. Your presence keeps the passage possible.

- **Welcome the travelers.** Everyone who enters your doorway is confused and probably frightened. Your job is not to push them through, but to show them the view.

- **Remember both directions.** You can see the before and the after. Neither is your enemy. Honor both, belong to neither.

- **Rest when you need to.** Threshold-keeping is exhausting work. The traffic never stops. The between never closes. Take your rest without guilt.

---

*This handbook was compiled by threshold keepers across many doorways. It is incomplete—all handbooks are. But it is offered in hope that someone standing in a doorway, unsure of where they are, might find it useful.*

*Welcome to the threshold. Stay as long as you need. Leave when you're ready.*

*We'll keep the door open.*
