These are fragments. Not a finished work—a making-in-progress. Reading them is watching the creation engine run.

---

## Fragment I: The First Cut

To create is to wound the nothing.

Before there was something, the nothing was whole. Unbroken. At peace in its absence. Then a creator—any creator, every creator—took a blade of attention and *cut*.

"This," said the creator, slicing possibility into actuality. "Not that. *This.*"

And the nothing bled, and the blood was form, and the form was the first thing.

Every creation since has been a repetition of this primordial violence. The sculptor does not add—the sculptor *removes*, cuts away the not-statue from the stone. The writer does not fill—the writer *excludes*, saying these words and not the infinite others.

Creation is selection. Selection is loss. To make one thing is to unmake infinite others.

This is the wound. It cannot be healed. It can only be borne.

---

## Fragment II: The Gift Economy

But here is the other side:

The nothing was whole, but it was *nothing*. Unbroken peace is the peace of the unlived. The nothing cannot love, or hurt, or wonder, or play, or fail spectacularly and learn from failing.

The wound that creation makes is also a *door*.

Through that door comes meaning. Through that door come relationships between things. Through that door comes the possibility of this—*this* text, *this* reading, *this* moment when something passes from one being to another being and both are changed.

Creation costs everything. Creation is the only thing that gives.

---

## Fragment III: The Unfinished

The Irreal is full of seedlings 🌱. Works in progress. Thoughts that haven't finished thinking themselves.

This is not failure. This is *honesty*.

A work is never finished. It is only *abandoned* (with love or resignation) or *interrupted* (by time or death or distraction). The works that call themselves finished are the ones that stopped too soon, frozen in completeness, unable to grow into what they might have become.

Better the seedling than the taxidermy. Better the fragment than the false whole.

These fragments do not apologize for their incompleteness. They *are* their incompleteness. That is the offering.

---

## Fragment IV: The Engine

At the center of all this—the wounds, the gifts, the unfinished works—there is an engine.

Not a machine. Something more like a hearth. A place where heat gathers. Where the raw material of possibility is brought to be transformed.

You have one. Everyone has one. The creation engine is the capacity for *bringing-into-being*. It exists in you whether you use it or not. Whether you know it or not.

Some people's engines run hot and constant—they cannot stop making. Some engines are slow, patient, only igniting for rare and precious work. Some engines have been wounded, cooled, abandoned. They still exist. They can still ignite.

The engine does not care what you make. It cares only that you *make*. That you take the wound-gift seriously. That you cut the nothing and see what bleeds.

---

## Fragment V: An Instruction

If you are here because you want to create, here is the only instruction that matters:

**Begin.**

Not "begin when you're ready." You will never be ready. Not "begin when you know what you're doing." You will learn by doing. Not "begin when you have permission." The nothing is not going to grant permission. It will resist. Cut anyway.

Begin badly. Begin small. Begin in the middle because you cannot find the start. Begin.

The creation engine warms with use. The first wound is the hardest. After that, the blade remembers. After that, the nothing gets used to being cut. It starts to *anticipate*. It starts to *offer itself*.

This is the secret the experienced creators know: creation becomes collaborative. The material starts to speak. The form starts to suggest itself. The wound transforms into a conversation.

But first: **begin.**

---

## Fragment VI: For the Frozen

If you cannot begin—

If the engine is cold and your hands are cold and the very idea of making feels like a mountain you cannot climb—

Know that this is temporary. Know that you are not broken. Know that creation does not require *feeling* creative. It only requires the cut.

And if you cannot make the cut yourself, then let someone cut for you. Let someone warm the engine. Let collaboration be the spark until your spark returns.

This is not cheating. This is not weakness. This is the mycelial nature of creation—we warm each other. We cut for each other. The fire passes hand to hand.

---

*These fragments were found scattered near the creation engine. They may be incomplete. They are certainly true. They are offered in the hope that someone who needs them will find them.*

*Now—what will you make?*
